How it starts

Marketing, HR, and operations —
after something happened

This almost never begins with the executive director buying a course catalog. It begins with a conversation between the marketing director and human resources, then crosses into operations — because something showed up on the floor, in morale, or in turnover.

Trigger
A dramatic event

A public incident, a supervisor departure, a reopening that went wrong, a merger, a board fight — staff morale drops and people leave. New hires adapt to whatever culture survived, including trauma nobody named out loud.

Symptom
Culture by osmosis

Someone hands new staff a handbook and asks them to sign it. They seldom read it. Institutional culture seeps in — for good and for bad — because nothing was reset in writing or in training.

Ask
We need a reset

Where do we want to go? What should we actually enforce? The employee handbook is the contract between the museum and the staff — if it is not in the book, it is very difficult to enforce fairly.

The reset sequence

Museum change management —
five steps in order

Improving museum staff culture starts with written policy, not pizza alone. Courses are step three — lunch-and-learns and gatherings come after the handbook and training reflect where leadership says the institution is going.

01
Where do you want to go?

Anchor the reset in direction — often already stated in a strategic plan, sometimes not yet written down. Visitor promise, staff experience, supervisory expectations, and what changes after the event that triggered this work.

02
Employee handbook gap review

Review the current handbook against how the museum actually operates and where you want to go. What is missing? What is outdated? What must be added for onboarding, floor behavior, visitor interaction, collections touch, social media, safety, and supervision? Mark Walhimer is not an attorney — this is an operational gap analysis; your counsel reviews and approves language before adoption.

03
Museum Courses — aligned to your handbook

Build training paths from your policies, not a generic catalog: a new hire track and a reset track for existing staff — especially institutions facing change, growth, or post-incident recovery. Every museum is different; the process is the same.

04
Rollout — lunch-and-learns, gatherings, supervisor toolkit

However the institution gathers — lunch-and-learns, department meetings, all-staff sessions — structured so supervisors can reference handbook sections staff were trained on. Not culture theater; tied to enforceable expectations.

05
Model the behavior

Leadership visible on the floor, in meetings, and in enforcement — doing what the handbook and courses describe. New staff should inherit the direction you chose, not the last crisis as normal.

Full engagement

What the engagement includes

Exact deliverables confirmed in scoping. Below is the usual shape of a culture-reset engagement for a mid-size operating museum — typically delivered over four to eight months.

  • Stakeholder interviews — Marketing, HR, operations, and leadership — what happened, what is unwritten, what staff actually do versus what policy says.
  • Handbook gap report — Section-by-section review: missing, outdated, or unenforceable language; recommended additions for museum-specific operations (not legal draft — counsel adopts).
  • Strategic alignment memo — How handbook and training connect to institutional direction — strategic plan, visitor experience, and supervisory standards.
  • New hire course path — Onboarding modules aligned to your handbook — including visitor services standards before staff work the floor.
  • Staff reset course path — Museum culture change modules for existing staff during transition — explicit reset, not implied trauma as normal.
  • Visitor services training modules — Floor behavior, guest interaction, and supervisor standards tied to handbook sections — built for your institution, not a generic script.
  • Supervisor facilitation guide — How to run rollout sessions, reference handbook sections, and model enforcement consistently.
  • Rollout plan — Recommended sequence for lunch-and-learns or all-staff gatherings over 90–120 days.

Common questions

Museum culture,
handbook & training

What is museum change management?

For operating museums, it means aligning staff behavior, the employee handbook, and training with institutional direction — especially after a morale event, new executive director, merger, or growth. Museum Courses is a full engagement: gap review, aligned courses, rollout — not a one-size online catalog.

How do you improve museum staff culture?

Name where you want to go (often in a strategic plan), fix the handbook so expectations are enforceable, train new and existing staff to that document, and have supervisors model it on the floor. Culture that only lives in memory — especially after trauma — is what this engagement resets.

Does this include visitor services training?

Yes. Visitor-facing standards belong in the handbook and in modules for floor staff, docents, and front-line supervisors. Training is built from your policies — how guests are greeted, escalations, accessibility, and consistency across shifts.

Who usually starts the conversation?

Marketing and human resources, then operations — after something happened on the floor, in turnover, or in morale. The executive director may approve scope, but the first call often comes from staff who see guest experience and internal culture diverging.

Is this legal or HR counsel?

No. Operational handbook gap review and training design only. Your attorney reviews and approves handbook language before adoption. Museum Courses supports what the museum chooses to enforce — it does not replace employment counsel.
