Why It Matters

A Vision Is Not a Plan. Strategy Is.

Too many museum strategic plans end their lives in a binder — elegant documents with no operating traction. Our practice draws on years inside institutions before Museum Planning LLC was founded in 1999; the plans we produce are built to be implemented, not filed. Every strategic priority is tested against budget realities, staffing, and capital requirements.

Principle 01
"Built to implement, not to file"
Strategic planning that doesn't connect to financial modeling produces wish lists. We develop mission and vision frameworks together with operating cost assumptions, revenue mix, and realistic implementation dependencies.
Principle 02
"The board owns the outcome"
The consultant facilitates; leadership owns the plan. Board interviews, staff workshops, and community listening sessions are structured to build buy-in during the process — not present it at the end.
Principle 03
"Sequence before space"
Strategic planning before a building or lease decision is finalized is often the right order. A grounded plan informs real estate, program mix, and capital timing — rather than architecture driving mission.

The plans we produce are designed to be built, not filed. We have been on the other side of the table when a consultant delivers a master plan. We know what happens to that document when the meeting ends.

Mark Walhimer · Managing Partner, Museum Planning LLC

Strategic Planning & Capital Campaign

When Fundraising Is Part of the Conversation

Many boards arrive asking for strategic planning and a comprehensive fundraising campaign in the same breath. That is a legitimate scope — and it requires clarity about what each piece does.

Strategic planning defines institutional direction: mission, priorities, and a multi-year roadmap. Capital campaign planning (Phase 02) structures how you raise the money — campaign goal, phasing, naming opportunities, and donor pyramid. The strategic plan sets the case; the campaign plan organizes the ask.

Integrated engagements — strategic plan plus relocation planning, museum feasibility study, or campaign architecture — often scope above the standard $45k–$75k range. Every engagement still begins with a one-hour conversation at no cost to define the right sequence.

Common Questions

Museum Strategic Planning — FAQ

Can you develop a strategic plan before a space is identified?

Yes. In fact, beginning strategic planning before a space decision is finalized is often the right sequence — a well-grounded plan can inform the real estate search rather than the other way around. We are comfortable working with uncertainty and building decision points around space selection as the process unfolds.

What does a museum strategic planning consultant do?

A museum strategic planning consultant works with leadership, board, and community stakeholders to define mission, vision, and values, then builds a multi-year plan with measurable goals, financial projections, and an implementation roadmap — including community assessment, peer benchmarking, and alignment with capital and operational realities.

How long does museum strategic planning take?

Typically 4–9 months depending on institutional complexity, stakeholder sessions required, and whether capital or relocation planning is integrated. The deliverable is a written strategic plan ready for board adoption.

What does museum strategic planning cost?

Standard strategic planning engagements typically range from $45,000 to $75,000 exclusive of travel. Integrated scopes — strategic plan plus relocation analysis, museum feasibility study, or capital campaign architecture — often run $75,000–$100,000+ depending on scale. Every engagement begins with a one-hour conversation at no cost.

What is the difference between a strategic plan and a master plan?

A strategic plan defines direction — mission, vision, priorities, and a multi-year roadmap. A museum master plan translates those goals into physical space and capital requirements. At Museum Planning LLC, the two are developed in relationship to one another.
