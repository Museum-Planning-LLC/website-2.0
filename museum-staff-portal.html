Employee Handbook Review

Your Handbook Is Your First Promise to Every Staff Member

Most small museums and city-operated cultural institutions have an employee handbook that was written once, approved once, and has not been meaningfully updated since. Federal and state leave law, harassment and discrimination requirements, and pay transparency rules have changed in the intervening years.

Our engagement begins with a full handbook audit — reviewing the current document against current federal and state requirements, identifying gaps and outdated language, and producing a revision coordinated with an employment attorney before it is distributed to staff.

Why Cities and Small Museums Specifically

Cities and counties that operate museums face a particular challenge: HR is often administered by a central municipal department that knows general employment law but does not know the museum context — part-time docents, seasonal educators, volunteers with functional staff roles.

Small independent museums face the mirror problem: deep institutional knowledge with thin HR infrastructure. The handbook gets written at opening, reviewed by a board member who is a lawyer (not an employment lawyer), and left unchanged for a decade.

Museum Staff Identity Portal

One Place Where the Whole Institution Lives

A museum's identity is only real if every person on staff can access it, reference it, and feel it applies to their work. For most small museums, that identity exists in a strategic plan the director has read and the security staff have never seen.

We build a branded, museum-specific digital environment — not a generic HR platform — where the employee handbook, mission and values, HR policies, and a staff suggestion channel live in one place, accessible from any device.

Shared Identity, Every Role

The most important function of the portal is not information delivery. It is shared identity. When the gallery educator and the operations manager both see the same mission statement the board just approved, they are part of the same institution in a way a PDF distribution list cannot create.

Before Any Portal Is Built

We ask the audit questions first:

Is your personnel handbook up to date? Has an attorney reviewed it within three years? Do all employees know it exists — and have acknowledged receipt in writing? Can every staff member state your museum's mission? Is your vision and values document current with your board's most recent strategic direction?

And critically: do your staff have a documented place to go with questions or suggestions — and does someone own the responsibility of responding?

These are not rhetorical questions. Each has a yes-or-no answer. For most small museums, the honest answers reveal gaps that a well-designed staff portal directly closes. The portal is not the starting point — the audit is.

Frequently Asked Questions

Questions About the Staff Portal Engagement

Does our museum's employee handbook need attorney review?

In most cases, yes — especially if the handbook has not been reviewed by an employment attorney in the past three years. Federal and state requirements for leave, harassment policy, pay practices, and at-will language change frequently. Our engagement begins with a handbook audit and coordinates attorney review before any portal work begins.

Is this only for city-operated museums, or independent nonprofits too?

Both. The engagement is designed for small and mid-size museums of all governance types — city-operated, county-operated, and independent nonprofits — where HR infrastructure is typically thin and the employee handbook has not been meaningfully updated in years. City museums face a specific challenge around generic municipal HR systems that don't account for the museum context. Independent nonprofits face a different version of the same gap.

What does a museum staff identity portal actually look like — is it software?

The portal is a branded, museum-specific digital environment — accessible from any device, requiring no app download, designed to feel like your museum rather than generic HR software. It houses the employee handbook, mission, vision, and values, HR policies, and a staff Q&A and suggestion channel in one place. It is built to be maintained by your staff without ongoing technical support.

What if our mission, vision, and values are already in a strategic plan?

Strategic plans are produced for boards and funders. Staff portals are built for staff. The language in a strategic plan often needs translation into something a front-of-house team member or security officer can use as a daily reference. Part of our engagement is reviewing whether existing mission, vision, and values language is accessible and current — and working with the director and board to update it if needed, before it goes into the portal.
