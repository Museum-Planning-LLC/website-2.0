Project Overview

Alcatraz Landing is the waterfront pre-visit experience at Pier 33 — where Alcatraz Cruises, LLC (Hornblower) boards thousands of visitors daily for the ferry to Alcatraz Island. Because ferry service operates under a National Park Service concession contract, NPS asked the concessionaire to provide interpretation before people board: context, maps, history, and orientation to the island's different periods and places.

Museum Planning, LLC was engaged by Alcatraz Cruises to design that arrival experience — including welcoming interpretive exhibits along the walk into the landing. The work followed Museum Planning's management of Alcatraz: Life on the Rock and applied the same concession-aware approach — meeting NPS expectations for public trust, accessibility, and inclusive welcome while delivering a practical visitor gateway at the pier.

The Challenge

Alcatraz Landing is both a queue and a threshold. Visitors arrive with uneven knowledge of the island — federal penitentiary, Native American occupation, military fort, natural refuge — and NPS needed the concessionaire to orient them before boarding. At the same time, the National Park Service made clear that the landing had to be ADA compliant and clearly welcoming to all visitors, not an afterthought squeezed into a ticket line.

Museum Planning's Role

Museum Planning designed the pre-visit experience and produced the drawings, graphics, tactile exhibits, audio systems, and outdoor signage that defined the landing. Content development required the same cross-stakeholder coordination as the touring exhibition — working with National Park Service staff, Alcatraz historians, and Alcatraz alumni to ensure every story on the pier was accurate before it reached the public. The work included:

  • Visitor experience and interpretive planning for the Pier 33 arrival sequence — including welcoming exhibits along the walk into the landing
  • Orientation to Alcatraz history, island geography, and the island's major historical eras
  • Content development and review with National Park Service staff, Alcatraz historians, and Alcatraz alumni
  • Outdoor signage — design, fabrication coordination, and installation for the landing environment
  • Exhibition layout drawings and CAD documentation for landing components
  • Tactile exhibits — including a tactile model of Alcatraz Island and tactile interpretation of the island's eras
  • Braille labeling and accessible graphic panels
  • Outdoor interpretive vignettes — including National Park Service and Native American occupation stories
  • An automated audio tour — triggered as visitors moved through the landing, activating the corresponding exhibits
  • Audio recording, equipment integration, and installation
  • Artifact display coordination (including the Champion artifact case)
  • Close collaboration with Lighthouse for the Blind — working with their volunteers and advisors to test exhibits and visitor experiences
  • ADA coordination with National Park Service staff — responding to NPS accessibility requirements under the concession contract

Historical Accuracy & Outdoor Signage

Because Alcatraz Landing sits on concession land but interprets a National Park Service site, the content had to satisfy both Alcatraz Cruises and NPS — and hold up to scrutiny from people who lived the history. Museum Planning worked directly with National Park Service staff, Alcatraz historians, and former inmates and prison guards from the alumni community to verify facts, language, and emphasis across panels, audio scripts, and tactile elements.

That same review process extended to the landing's outdoor signage — the visible graphic system visitors encounter as they approach and move through Pier 33. Signage had to orient, welcome, and instruct in a high-traffic waterfront environment while staying faithful to the island's layered history.

Accessibility & Inclusive Design

NPS priority for this project was accessibility and welcome. Museum Planning did not treat ADA as a checklist item at the end of design — accessibility was built into the experience from the start, with user testing throughout.

Museum Planning worked closely with Lighthouse for the Blind, their volunteers, and their advisors to test the landing's exhibits and experiences. That partnership shaped the tactile island model, tactile era panels, braille systems, and audio tour — ensuring that blind and low-vision visitors could orient themselves to Alcatraz before boarding the ferry.

The automated audio tour extended that inclusive approach to the full arrival path: as visitors walked through the landing, the system triggered narration and description at each exhibit — so orientation happened in motion, not only at fixed reading stations. The landing was conceived as an inclusive threshold: readable, navigable, tactile, audible, and open to the full range of visitors who come to Pier 33.

Outcome

Alcatraz Landing gave Alcatraz Cruises a concession-compliant pre-visit experience that met NPS expectations for interpretation and accessibility at the San Francisco waterfront. The project extended Museum Planning's Alcatraz work from the national touring exhibition to the daily arrival experience for millions of island visitors.
