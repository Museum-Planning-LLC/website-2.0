Project Overview

Alcatraz: Life on the Rock is a traveling exhibition created by Alcatraz Cruises, LLC — the Hornblower concessionaire for ferry service to Alcatraz Island — as both a marketing program and a contractual obligation with the National Park Service. Alcatraz is a National Park Service historic site held in public trust; the island is administered by NPS, while visitor ferry and related services operate under concession contract.

Mark Walhimer, managing partner of Museum Planning, LLC, was engaged by Alcatraz Cruises to launch and manage the national tour. He knew the exhibition from earlier work as chief operating officer at Academy Studios, where the show was originally developed. When the tour had not yet opened and NPS expectations were rising, Museum Planning stepped in to read the concession requirements, build budgets, sell and book venues, hire transportation and installation crews, and steward the artifact collection through each stop — while extending the exhibition with new interactives, fabrication, evaluation, school-group materials, and video production for the tour.

The Challenge

A traveling exhibition tied to a federal concession contract is not a standard museum rental. The concessionaire needed the tour running — on schedule, insured, and compliant — while NPS staff needed confidence that artifacts, interpretation, and accessibility met park standards. Museum Planning had to work across both contexts: the for-profit concession side where the contract and invoices lived, and the nonprofit park side where public trust and federal expectations applied.

Museum Planning's Role

From February 2011 through 2014, Mark Walhimer served as project manager for Life on the Rock. The work included:

  • Reviewing the concession contract and translating requirements into schedules, budgets, and deliverables
  • Sales and promotion — marketing the exhibition to museums, visitor centers, fairs, and public venues nationwide
  • Venue booking, logistics, and three-month touring cycles — including hiring transportation for moves between venues
  • Exhibition design and fabrication oversight for new and revised components
  • Adding interactive exhibits beyond the original Academy Studios scope
  • Exhibition evaluation — assessing visitor experience and interpretive effectiveness across tour stops
  • Educational materials for school groups and field trips
  • Video production for the traveling exhibition and companion programs — including the tour overview film and Alcatraz: The Last Day
  • Alumni events — meeting former inmates and prison guards, documenting their stories in video, photography, and oral histories
  • Hiring and supervising install and takedown crews at each venue
  • Insurance certificates for venues and lenders — including NPS sites
  • Artifact stewardship as curator and registrar: condition reports, loan agreements, travel with the collection, and sign-off at each venue
  • ADA coordination with National Park Service staff for the traveling exhibition

Alumni Events & Oral Histories

Beyond the traveling exhibition itself, Museum Planning organized alumni programs that brought former Alcatraz inmates and prison guards together with the public at tour stops. Mark Walhimer met with alumni, documented their stories, and produced video, photography, and oral history materials — including work around Alcatraz: The Last Day and programs at Ellis Island and other venues.

Tour Venues

Confirmed tour stops during Museum Planning's management included:

  • Ellis Island — opening October 2011 (ribbon cutting October 1)
  • Central Railroad of New Jersey Terminal, Liberty State Park — February–July 2012
  • San Mateo County Fair — June 2013
  • Yuma Quartermaster Depot State Historic Park — January–April 2013
  • Hyatt Regency San Francisco — April–July 2013
  • Hyatt Regency Santa Clara — November 2013–February 2014
  • Oakland Marriott — July–September 2014
  • Sheraton at the Falls — June–August 2014

Outcome

The exhibition opened at Ellis Island and traveled to venues across the country. NPS staff, Alcatraz Cruises, and Hornblower were satisfied with the launch and ongoing tour operations. The engagement led to additional Alcatraz-related projects — including work on Alcatraz Landing, documented separately.
