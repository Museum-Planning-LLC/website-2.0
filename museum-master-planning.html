Why It Matters

Not a Rendering. A Plan.

A master plan is not an architect's vision for what the building could look like. It is a rigorous document that defines what the museum needs — in square feet, program adjacencies, budget, and phasing — before architects begin drawing. The plans we produce are built around operational sustainability, not just construction viability.

Principle 01
"Built to be built, not filed"
We have been on the other side of the table when a consultant delivers a master plan. We know what happens to that document when the meeting ends — and we structure deliverables so boards, architects, and campaign chairs can actually use them.
Principle 02
"Feasibility before floor plans"
Skipping feasibility to get to design faster is the single most expensive mistake in museum development. We confirm viability before programming space — or we tell you honestly when the sequence needs to pause.
Principle 03
"Campaign structure included"
A master plan without a fundable capital campaign structure is incomplete. We produce campaign phasing, naming tiers, and donor pyramid framing alongside building program and budget.

Master Plan & Capital Campaign

The Plan Tells You What to Build and How to Fund It

A capital campaign raises money for a specific, defensible vision. The master plan produces the campaign structure: total goal, phasing, naming opportunities, and donor pyramid — alongside building program and construction budget.

We do not deliver a master plan that stops at square footage without a fundable campaign architecture. Donors fund stories anchored in financial models that survive due diligence — and the master plan is where that story gets its numbers.

When institutional direction still needs clarity first, see museum strategic planning. When viability is still unconfirmed, start with feasibility.

Common Questions

Museum Master Planning — FAQ

What does a museum master plan include?

Building program, architectural program, conceptual floor plans, phasing strategy, project budget (construction and exhibitions), and capital campaign structure. It translates mission and strategic vision into physical space requirements and the financial plan to realize them.

How much does museum master planning cost?

Typically starting at $100,000 plus travel. Scope covers building program through capital campaign structure. Every engagement begins with a one-hour conversation at no cost and no obligation.

What is the difference between a feasibility study and a master plan?

A feasibility study ($40k–$70k) validates viability before capital is committed. A master plan ($100k+) comes after feasibility confirms the project can survive — and defines what to build and how to fund it. Skipping feasibility is the most common and most expensive mistake we see.

Does a museum need a master plan before hiring an architect?

Yes. The building program and architectural program define what the architect is designing. Without them, the architect defines the program by default — typically producing a building that costs more to construct and operate than necessary.

How long does museum master planning take?

Typically 6–12 months depending on scope, stakeholder engagement, and institutional complexity. Projects move faster when feasibility and strategic planning are already complete.
