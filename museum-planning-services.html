How we think about it

It is not that complicated — if the documents line up and the museum is woven into its community.

When someone calls and says they need a feasibility study, the first question is feasibility of what: approximate square footage, audience, voice of the museum, field trips, school groups, community partners. Right-size the building. Capture families and you are on your way.

Museums that struggle are not woven into the local community. A city can stretch toward a new science center, children's museum, or digital art museum — but it still has to be of the community.

If the pro forma says 20,000 square feet and the master plan describes 60,000, you have a mismatch — and that will not work. Exhibition design is the expression of feasibility, pro forma, and master plan together.

Every study produces boards and drawings you can react to — not words alone. Renderings for voice and experience. Diagrams for audiences. Floor plans for scale. Maps for the whole cultural ecosystem. Getting there takes workshops, community conversations, and time listening to why certain things might work and others might not.

Each engagement has a visual deliverable clients can point at and say this — or not this.

01 · Numbers

Museum feasibility studies

Do the numbers work? Audience, scale, operating model, and community demand — before capital is committed. We answer feasibility of what, not a generic template. Experience renderings ask: clean and minimal, homie like your living room, or an elevated upscale visit?

02 · Experience

Museum master planning

The pro forma and the visitor experience have to match. Master planning maps what the feasibility study says is viable into building program, phasing, and the experience visitors will actually have — almost always as a floor plan.

03 · Expression

Museum exhibition design

Exhibition design is how feasibility, financial model, and master plan become physical — a detailed floor plan with exhibits named, sequenced, and scoped for fabrication.

04 · Audiences

Museum strategic planning

Who you serve and how you reach them — field trips, community groups, membership, events. Strategy diagrams map personas, channels, and programming. Museums are event-based; this is how you drive repeat visitation when year-three attendance starts to slip.

05 · Ecosystem

City cultural plans

For cities: the buildings, institutions, audiences, and constituents — mapped so cultural assets can thrive together. Downtown maps show museums, arts centers, heritage sites, and the connections between them. Sometimes that includes strengthening what belongs in the ecosystem and culling what does not.

Build for the third year, not the opening.

Common Questions

Museum Consultants — FAQ

What does a museum consultant do?
Align feasibility, master planning, strategic planning, and exhibition design — so pro forma, visitor experience, and community reach match. We lead every engagement from first conversation through opening and year-three operations.
When should we hire museum consultants?
Before capital is committed — when a board, city, or donor group needs an honest answer about viability, audience, or what to build. The usual sequence runs feasibility, then strategic planning, then master planning — each document building on the last.
How much do museum consultants cost?
Feasibility studies typically run $40k–$70k, strategic planning $45k–$75k, and master planning from $100k, exclusive of travel. Every engagement begins with a one-hour conversation at no cost.
What is the difference between museum consultants and architects?
Consultants define program, audience, and financial viability before design begins. Architects translate a confirmed building program into physical design — the master plan connects the two.
