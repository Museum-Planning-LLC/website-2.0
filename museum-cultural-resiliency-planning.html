The Situation

Planning for institutions that have shifted from growth to stewardship.

Most planning frameworks assume expansion — a new wing, a bigger campaign, a larger footprint. That is not the moment most cultural institutions and municipalities are in. Operating budgets are flat or frozen. Grant funding is uncertain. Staff are stretched. Deferred maintenance keeps compounding whether or not there is a plan for it.

What still exists is defensive capital: HVAC, envelope, code compliance, consolidation, and the hard decisions about which spaces to keep running, which to rest, and in what order. Resiliency Planning maps that terrain — clinically, without politics, and in a form a board or council can actually act on.

Is this on your desk right now? A building that costs more to run than it should, decisions about closed galleries or underused wings, and no clear sequence for what to fund first?

Footprint pressureUnderused wings, mothballed galleries, and square footage that no longer matches attendance or staffing.
Deferred maintenanceHVAC, envelope, and systems work that keeps getting pushed — and more expensive — every budget cycle.
Frozen capitalA board or council willing to fund the right project, but no defensible answer to "which one, and why first?"
Portfolio complexityCities and counties carrying multiple cultural assets — museums, libraries, historic properties — with no unified read.
The Engagement

The Resiliency Planning Study.

A fixed-scope study that maps your footprint, operations, and phased capital options — and delivers them as a menu of discrete, fundable projects your board or council can act on one item at a time.

Fixed Scope Fixed Fee Fixed Schedule Defined Deliverable

The Study is Phase 1 of a capital decision — not open-ended consulting. It begins with document intake (990s, plans, utility data, org chart), moves through two to three days on-site walking the footprint and interviewing operations staff, and concludes with a board-ready planning document four to five weeks after contract signature.

Because it is scoped as a discrete capital study, it fits a CapEx or CIP budget line — not an operating budget that has no room in it.

The Deliverable

The Modular Implementation Menu.

One board-ready document. Not a vision book that sits on a shelf — a sequenced menu of discrete capital items, each with a rough cost band, that can be funded independently as money becomes available.

01 · Baseline
Institutional baseline
Financial read, regional demographics, and competitive context — where the institution actually stands, in numbers.
02 · Footprint
Footprint analysis
Utilization by zone, underused space, and mothball / reactivate options with their operating implications.
03 · Operations
Operations map
Circulation, staffing workflows, ticketing, and public access patterns — how the building and the team actually move.
04 · Systems
Building systems
HVAC zones, utilities, envelope condition, and deferred maintenance priorities, ranked by risk and cost.
05 · Capital Menu
Phased capital menu
Discrete, fundable line items with rough cost bands — sized so a board or council can approve them one at a time.
06 · Sequence
Recommended sequence
What to do first, what to defer, and — just as important — what not to fund at all.
Downstream · Optional
A path forward when items are funded
When your board or council funds a menu item, we can support implementation as owner's representative. That is a downstream option, never a requirement — the Study is designed to stand on its own.

The Method

One holistic read across space, money, staff, and mission.

A museum is a single ecosystem, not a set of siloed problems. The Study reads all of it together — which is why the recommendations hold up in a board meeting.

—

Financial position. Institutional and Form 990 read: revenue mix, expense structure, and reserves.

—

Community context. Regional demographics and the competitive landscape for attention and attendance.

—

Space & circulation. Floor plans, visitor flow, and how the public actually moves through the building.

—

Building systems. HVAC, utilities, envelope, and footprint utilization — where the operating money goes.

—

Revenue & access. Ticketing, earned revenue, and public access patterns.

—

Staffing & workflows. Where staff reductions have landed hardest, and where smart-building and administrative tools can quietly absorb routine load.

—

Capital options. Phased capital items with rough cost bands, ready for the menu.
