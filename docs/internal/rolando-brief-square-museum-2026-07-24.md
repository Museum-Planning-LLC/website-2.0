## What this is

We need a small set of architectural renderings for a **generic** local history museum on a **Southern town square** — the type of project mayors and city councils ask about when they say *“what’s the future of museums?”*

This is the **anti–Dateland** image: daylight, objects, community, downtown life — smart and contemporary, but **civic and sustainable**, not a black-box spectacle at $85/ticket.

You did excellent work on **Howard**; that tone and clarity are the reference. I also opened **C.O. Polk Interactive Museum** in McDonough, GA (2017 exhibit package on file — five program zones, touch tables, kids/maker, square connection). This brief **generalizes** that logic for a **~10,000 ft²** building where a city doesn’t yet have a Polk.

## Five zones (must read in plan + at least one interior)

These match our standard “kit” — same spine we use on real projects:

1. **Entry / lobby** — welcome desk, “today’s activities,” connection to tourism/downtown; natural light  
2. **Main gallery** — local history, timeline, **objects in cases**, one touch/interactive moment (not wall-to-wall screens)  
3. **Theater / multi-use** — community meetings, school groups, weekend programs; chairs storable  
4. **Art / changing gallery** — local art + history; museum-quality cases; one changing-exhibition wall  
5. **Kids / maker** — bright, safe, **3D print or maker table**, reading nook, post-office or play element tied to local story  

**Back of house (plan or cutaway):** collection storage, prep, staff workroom, loading/service — visibly ** proportional** to 5k BoH (not omitted).

---

## What to show in people and activity

Renderings should feel **alive on a Saturday morning**:

- Families and seniors — diverse, regional, not stock-photo generic  
- Kids at a touch table or maker bench  
- **Natural light** — windows, clerestory, or courtyard; avoid cave-like black box  
- **Real objects** — cases, artifacts, local photography; screens support story, don’t replace it  
- **Weekend event energy** — optional exterior: festival tent, foot traffic on square, museum as anchor  
- **Downtown** — café, courthouse, shops at square edge; museum pulls people into core  

---

## What to avoid

- Dateland / TeamLab / pure projection cave with no collection  
- Endless video walls with no objects  
- Generic “science center” trope without local history anchor  
- Luxury materials that read as $200M campaign — this is **civic budget credibility**  
- Empty lobby “jewel box” with no staff, storage, or back-of-house honesty
