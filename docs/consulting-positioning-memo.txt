After Dean’s bankruptcy, shop fabricators Mark worked with as COO

they would pay rent after materials/equipment consumed the $60K. Felt

Lesson: contract revenue ≠ solvency; capital expenditure on tools/space
without operating reserve kills small shops — same family as 500K
attendance / wrong-sized building. Mark’s “solid” question was correct;
kindness is naming rent, not only celebrating install quality.
• Public site: credibility via projects and methodology — not “we made
  millions”; fees/ranges only if Mark approves specific numbers.

----------------------------------------------------------------
ONE LINE
----------------------------------------------------------------
We sell phased institutional judgment — feasibility of WHAT, strategy as
the receipt, master plan only after instruments exist — not theater,
not arbitrary exhibits, not campus plans without floor plans.

----------------------------------------------------------------
WHY CONSULTING (vs. ED / exhibit design)
----------------------------------------------------------------
• Nonprofit ED: absorb scope (parking, mail, neighborliness) without
  an invoice; tone-policed when naming payroll, law, or asset risk.
• Exhibit design alone: arbitrary without strategy; board favors
  (donor garage, uncle’s $20K) override design with no shared outcome.
• Provider / consulting: “Out of scope — here’s the fee.” Package ends;
  next step optional. Protects margin and truth (when owners don’t
  give work away for likability).

Art = Mark’s authorship and communication. Consulting = paid operator
people hire to name reality.

----------------------------------------------------------------
FEASIBILITY OF WHAT (lived example — Liberty Science Center)
----------------------------------------------------------------
• Third-party feasibility (White Oak) modeled ~500,000 first-year
  attendance from NYC market; opening reality ~250,000.
• 250K is strong; building and staffing were scaled for 500K.
• Second year: layoffs — shop staff Mark knew lost jobs.
• Lesson in bones: feasibility must state ASSUMPTIONS, CAPACITY, and
  “what we are feasibility-ing” — not a number borrowed from the wrong
  geography or peer. Wrong feasibility → wrong capital → real people
  unemployed.

Never publish vendor blame on the website; use as internal discipline
for how Museum Planning LLC runs studies.

----------------------------------------------------------------
INSTRUMENTS BEFORE MASTER PLAN (Hunter-shaped pattern)
----------------------------------------------------------------
RFQs often ask for campus master plan with no:
  strategic refresh since opening, audience surveys, audit rhythm,
  sound employee handbook, floor plans, annual reports.

Correct sequence:
  1) Baseline + audience + board/stakeholder + benchmarks (e.g. multi-
     month, six-figure engagement when scope is real).
  2) Strategic frame — outcomes agreed.
  3) Master plan / capital only if data supports it.

Portfolio-scale alternative: ~$100K map — what to sell, what to protect
for visitors, what building matters; next phase scoped separately.

----------------------------------------------------------------
STRATEGY AS RECEIPT (exhibit design)
----------------------------------------------------------------
Without adopted outcomes, “no” to arbitrary additions is personal.
With strategic plan (e.g. community involvement, Main Street employers),
exhibition choices are tested against agreed results — not taste.

----------------------------------------------------------------
FACILITATION METHOD (vision / options)
----------------------------------------------------------------
Before arguing one future, align artifacts:
  renderings, floor plans, mission/vision/values, vision boards.

Show the audience two concrete futures (A vs B). Often they say:
  not A, not B, A+B, or add C. That clarifies feasibility of WHAT
  before capital or design commitment.

LIVED OPERATIONS — Children’s Museum of Indianapolis (PM role)
----------------------------------------------------------------
Hired as project manager (not shop manager) on a major exhibit project
~two years behind schedule. Designer vs. shop conflict; long-tenured
fabricator vs. new staff — interpersonal deadlock, nothing buildable.

Mark’s move (same as always):
  floor plan first → exhibit areas → potential costs → budget alignment
  → in-house capabilities inventory. Full baseline before build.

Staff anger at visibility into schedule/budget/reality. Opening day:
  Mark vacuuming gallery pre-opening; staff member enters with puppets,
  hand-drawn cardboard sign, places “exhibit” on floor minutes before
  public entry. Mark objects (“what is that — people are waiting outside”).
  Fired for that moment.

Related pattern (Indianapolis / press): gala and opening events scheduled;
press engaged (NY Observer, local media); floor plan set; exhibits
installed — then staff ask to postpone opening ~two weeks to finish
puppets / personal work. On ~$2M budget, last-minute scope is an insult
to installed work, public date, and PM baseline. Happens often in museums.

Lesson: PM and contractor discipline threaten opaque culture; opening
theater tolerates last-minute scope; person who enforces opening standard
is ejected. Reinforces consulting OUTSIDE staff role — scoped engagement,
written deliverables, not vacuuming while unpaid scope appears on floor.

LIVED OPERATIONS — CMOM exhibit shop (Children’s Museum of Manhattan)
----------------------------------------------------------------
Hired as exhibit manager; small basement shop outside early childhood
area. Shop unusable: ~30 years of accumulation — not broken tools, junk
(empty boxes, debris); table saw and chop saw inaccessible.

• Ordered dumpster in courtyard; delivery blocked — ~2 ft of pizza
  boxes already in courtyard.
• Unknown history: birthday parties on 2nd floor ~20 years; high school
  staff threw pizza boxes out windows into courtyard.
• Rats in pizza boxes; hired crew to clear courtyard before dumpster.
• Cleaned shop; told education staff: no pizza out window; museum not
  picking that up again.

Lesson: operations debt is often invisible until someone tries a normal
facilities action; education programming had been externalizing mess
for decades.

----------------------------------------------------------------
OPERATING MUSEUM REALITY (why staff portal / handbook matter)
----------------------------------------------------------------
Pattern repeats: assets used without notice (parking), scope creep
(mail/packages), illegal or sloppy HR (blanket exempt), physical
risk (e.g. water in collection storage). Boards want attendance but
undermine visitor parking or operations.

Staff portal + handbook audit = operating layer — mission/values staff
can use, attorney-reviewed policies, not culture theater alone.

----------------------------------------------------------------
LIVED OPERATIONS — Museum of Arts and Sciences, Macon (~2024–2025)
----------------------------------------------------------------
What happened (pattern): payroll, FLSA, water in collection storage,
parking used without notice, mail/packages scope creep, news frames
(“ripped poster”) — operator naming reality while board/city want
performed calm.

What I should've done — field trip / shared understanding:

Precedent (Macon already had this playbook): Leadership Macon — Greater
Macon Chamber of Commerce’s year-long civic leadership program. Class
days put cross-sector stakeholders on buses and site visits so the room
shares one picture of the community. Smart model; I should have run
the same play for the museum, not assumed the board would absorb a
transformation they never saw in person.

Also precedent: Community Foundation of Central Georgia ran workshops
for local nonprofits — outside experts (e.g. managers of major capital
campaigns, legal/regulatory updates) brought the room up to a shared
baseline. Found those very helpful; same idea as Tellus: witnesses and
vocabulary before big asks, not strategy in a vacuum.

Before carrying transformation alone, charter one day: bus the board
of directors, Leadership Macon–style cohort (or invite key alumni /
Chamber leadership), mayor (or designee), Knight Foundation contact
(if engaged), Community Foundation of Central Georgia, Peyton Anderson
Foundation (major MAS donor), and key staff to Tellus Science Museum
(Cartersville — comparable peer, not Macon tourism). Where a bus is
not enough, partner with CFCG-style workshops — capital campaign
reality, compliance, operating law — so board and funders hear the
same expert frame before committing or blocking.

Not a junket. One structured message in the room:

  “This is not exactly what I want to build — but it is close. I
  think we can get here. I am going to need all of your support to
  do it. If I do not have that support, it is time for me to leave.”

Let them see the same reference institution, same gap, same capital
and programming ask — so “no” or “yes” is collective, not personal.

Lesson: ED turnaround fails when board, city, chamber, and funders
never share one physical picture of what we are feasibility-ing — and
never sit in the same room for expert briefings (field trip or CFCG-
style workshop) before capital or compliance decisions.
Strategy-as-receipt needs witnesses, not only a strategic-plan PDF.
